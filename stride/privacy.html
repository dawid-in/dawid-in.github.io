Stride: Personal Bests

Privacy Policy

Last updated 27 October 2026

In short: Stride has no account, no servers, no analytics, no ads and no tracking. Your health and workout data stays on your iPhone and Apple Watch. The developer never receives it.

Data Stride reads

With your permission, Stride reads the following from Apple Health: workouts and their GPS routes, heart rate, steps, active calories, walking and running distance, and exercise minutes. Stride only reads this data; it never writes to or changes Apple Health. You choose which types to share, and can change that at any time in the Health app: tap your profile picture, then Apps → Stride.

If you use the Apple Watch app, it asks for its own permission to read today's steps, active calories, walking and running distance and exercise minutes from Apple Health on the watch.

If you choose to import a Garmin Connect activities export (a CSV file), Stride reads that file and keeps a copy of the activities it contains on your iPhone. You can remove the import at any time in Settings.

Where your data is kept

Everything Stride reads or calculates is stored only on your devices: in the app's own storage on your iPhone, in a storage area shared with Stride's widgets on the same iPhone, and on your Apple Watch, which reads today's totals from Apple Health on the watch and receives your personal bests from the iPhone directly. Stride does not upload your data anywhere, and nobody else, including the developer, can access it.

Place names

To show where a workout started (for example "Hyde Park, London"), Stride sends the starting coordinate of the workout's route to Apple's map service to look up a place name. This request is handled by Apple under Apple's privacy policy; it is not linked to you or sent to the developer. The resulting name is saved on your iPhone so it isn't looked up again.

Supporter purchase

Stride offers an optional one-off in-app purchase, "Stride Supporter", that supports the developer and unlocks extra widgets, Apple Watch face complications and alternate app icons. The purchase is handled entirely by Apple through the App Store, under Apple's privacy policy. Stride never sees your name, Apple ID, payment details or email address. To know whether you are a supporter, Stride asks the App Store on your device; the developer only receives the anonymous sales reports Apple provides to all developers. Your iPhone tells your Apple Watch and Stride's widgets whether you are a supporter, so they can unlock; this stays on your devices.

Reminders and ratings

Stride may remind you about Stride Supporter a few days and weeks after you install it, and may show Apple's standard "rate this app" prompt once you've used it on several days. To time these, Stride keeps the date you first opened it and a count of the days you've used it, on your iPhone only. Ratings and reviews go to Apple under Apple's privacy policy.

Widgets and watch complications

Stride's widgets and Apple Watch complications show today's totals, your personal bests and your top workouts. They read this from storage shared between Stride and its widgets on the same device, and today's totals directly from Apple Health. Nothing is sent anywhere.

What Stride does not do

  • No account or sign-in.
  • No analytics, crash reporting SDKs, advertising or tracking.
  • No selling or sharing of data with anyone.
  • No subscriptions; the only purchase is the optional, one-off Supporter purchase.
  • Health data is never used for advertising or marketing, and never stored in iCloud by Stride.

Deleting your data

Deleting Stride from your iPhone and Apple Watch removes everything it stored. Your data in Apple Health is not affected.

Children

Stride does not knowingly collect any personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new date.
